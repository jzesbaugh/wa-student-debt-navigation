# Post-patch review: “fake peanut gallery”

These are role-based review passes of the finished static site, not statements from outside reviewers.

## Legislator / policy staffer
- Understands the problem quickly: this is a handoff/retention gap, not a debt-forgiveness campaign.
- Motivated by the “Summons Test” because it asks for a concrete systems check instead of prescribing a bill.
- Strongest addition: the FAFSA analogy shows that navigation gaps can be improved without creating an entirely new bureaucracy.
- Remaining caution: keep the site clear that the 2027 notice is progress, not proof of failure.

## College administrator / trustee
- Sees an actionable institutional question: if a student brings court papers tomorrow, what does staff do?
- The warm-referral model feels operationally feasible and avoids asking colleges to become law offices.
- Strongest addition: “Do not assume there is a parent to call.” It connects vulnerable-student support to the existing retention mission.
- Remaining caution: avoid implying every campus has Student Legal Services; use “where available.”

## Benefits Hub coach / Basic Needs Navigator
- Clear about the boundary between navigation and legal advice.
- The one screening question and referral tree are usable.
- The FAFSA dependency-override experience feels familiar and helps explain why a student may need active navigation.
- Remaining caution: frontline staff need local referral details; a future downloadable one-page campus-specific referral sheet would help.

## Student / first-time reader
- Understands that the site is not blaming them for debt.
- “Get Help” is easy to find and the parent/family-safety-net point feels realistic.
- Potential confusion: the advocacy material is dense if they arrived in crisis; keeping Get Help visually separate and prominent is important.

## Creditor-side skeptic / defense lawyer
- Harder to dismiss because the site concedes valid judgments can be enforced, acknowledges the court ruled against Jesse on accounting, and recognizes recent legislative reforms.
- The BECU campus-partner section is strongest when framed as an institutional handoff question, not hypocrisy or motive.
- Remaining caution: never convert the 765-account illustration into a claim about students or BECU delinquency.

## Communications editor
- The project now has a coherent arc: retention → hidden/slow-burning debt → court trigger → handoff → feasible action.
- The personal material supports rather than dominates the argument.
- Most important CTA: “Run the Summons Test.”
- No major conceptual hole remains.

## Overall landing
The site now gives each audience a clear next step:
- Student: Get qualified help now.
- Navigator: Recognize, warm-refer, keep supporting retention.
- College: Test the referral pathway and train frontline staff.
- Legislator / regulator: Examine the handoff, information, and data gaps around systems that already exist.
- Financial partner: Explain distress/hardship pathways and how campus partnerships connect members to help.
