About this project

I’ve sat on both sides of the desk.

I was a Benefits Hub Coach helping students navigate financial problems while I was also a student carrying a consumer judgment of my own.

Preamble

Debt did not begin as a legal theory.

My house burned down. Later, my video business failed over the course of two economic downturns. That is the short version of how financial pressure entered my life.

I am not asking this project to relitigate those years or turn them into a sympathy story. They are context for something much more ordinary: people can enter consumer debt through disruption, bad timing, and economic loss, then carry the legal consequences for years.

By 2022–23, I was serving as an AmeriCorps Benefits Hub Coach at UW Bothell and Cascadia College, helping students find resources intended to keep financial instability from interrupting their education.

Why I am telling it

I made it to graduation.

My concern is the student who doesn’t.

I’m not telling confidential student stories. I’m using my own public record and hypothetical students to ask whether the systems Washington already built connect when the problem becomes consumer-debt litigation.

What coaching taught me

Not every student has a parent to call.

Some of the hardest work I did as a Benefits Hub Coach involved students who did not have a usable relationship with their parents. Sometimes there had been abandonment or estrangement. Sometimes contact with family was unsafe. Sometimes the student was simply navigating adulthood without the family infrastructure many people assume an eighteen- or twenty-year-old has.

At the time I served in 2022–23, getting a student recognized as independent for federal financial-aid purposes could require a case-by-case dependency override supported by documentation, and the determination generally had to be revisited each year. Part of my role was helping students understand that a pathway existed and helping them reach the people who could make the determination. Source S23

I did not make those decisions. I helped students navigate toward them.

Why that matters here

A right on paper is not the same as a usable path.

Federal rules have since improved this process. Students with unusual circumstances can now indicate that on the FAFSA, receive provisional independent status while a school reviews the situation, and—once approved at the same institution—generally do not have to re-prove the same circumstances every year unless something changes or conflicting information appears. Source S24

That evolution is relevant to this project: systems can improve when policymakers recognize where navigation breaks down.

What this project is—and isn’t

It is

An awareness project about student retention, navigation, consumer debt, and practical handoffs between existing systems.

It is not

A campaign for debt forgiveness, an argument that valid judgments should be unenforceable, a claim that every indebted student will drop out, or an instruction for campus staff to practice law.

Washington Student Debt Navigation

An awareness project about student retention, consumer debt, and the handoff between systems that already exist.
