# WA Student Debt Navigation

**Live site:** [WAStudentDebt.org](https://wastudentdebt.org/)

> **The resources exist. The handoff isn't automatic.**

WAStudentDebt.org is a Washington-focused public-interest project about what happens when ordinary consumer debt follows a college student into court—and whether the student-support systems already designed to keep that student enrolled know how to connect them to qualified legal help.

Washington has resources for students facing food insecurity, housing instability, financial-aid problems, and other basic-needs crises. Consumer protections, court procedures, and legal-aid resources also exist. This project looks at the space between those systems.

## What this project asks

When an enrolled student receives a debt-collection summons, judgment, or garnishment notice:

- Will they recognize that time may matter?
- Do they know where to go for qualified help?
- Can a campus navigator make a warm referral without practicing law?
- Does anyone see the legal problem as a student-retention problem before it becomes an academic one?

## What's on the site

- **Home** — the student-retention and navigation argument
- **How It Happens** — hypothetical student pathways illustrating how ordinary consumer debt can move through the Washington civil-debt system
- **Get Help** — direct links to authoritative Washington resources
- **For Navigators** — recognition and warm-referral guidance for student-support staff
- **For Decision Makers** — the Summons Test, systems questions, and feasible handoff gaps
- **About** — author background, documented case chronology, and why the question emerged
- **Glossary & Sources** — definitions, methodology, AI disclosure, and cited sources

## Why I built it

The project grew partly out of my own experience carrying a consumer judgment through college and later serving as an AmeriCorps Benefits Hub Coach at UW Bothell and Cascadia College.

That experience made me wonder how the students I worked with handled collection calls, lawsuits, and garnishment notices—particularly students with little money, limited family support, or no previous experience with the court system.

My personal experience is the reason I noticed the question. It is not the evidence for the answer.

## Important distinctions

- Sam, Maya, and Priya are hypothetical; they are not composites of students I coached.
- My own case is presented separately and is based on my records and the public court record.
- The project does not advocate debt forgiveness or argue that valid judgments should be unenforceable.
- It does not claim that every indebted student will leave school or file bankruptcy.
- Campus staff should recognize legal triggers and make qualified referrals, not practice law.
- The website provides general public information, not legal advice.

## Sources and review

Legal, institutional, and statistical claims are checked against primary or authoritative sources and cited on the site. Because laws and resources change, the Sources page displays a **Last reviewed** date.

## AI transparency

AI tools assisted with research organization, drafting, editing, and some illustrations. AI output is not treated as a factual source. Human review determines what is published, and factual claims are checked against cited sources before publication.
